# Results summary: S2 to S5 (story #9)

Generated by `scripts/summary_tables.py` from `results/2026-11-11`. Primary numbers are k6 client-side distributions side by side with `direct` (D18); percentiles are never subtracted.

Host: Apple M1, Docker Desktop 8 CPUs / 8.2 GB. Mock knobs: MOCK_ITL_MS=5, MOCK_LATENCY_MS=60, MOCK_OUTPUT_TOKENS=40, MOCK_TTFT_MS=20. Gateway container limit 2 CPU / 2 GB; tuned values per D25 (LiteLLM Granian with 2 workers, Bifrost and Langfuse defaults).

## What these tables are based on

- Profile `minimal` for every gateway (`direct` has no profile). Tuned values per D25. One gateway stack at a time, quiet Docker VM (metadata lists no other running containers for any of these runs), repo clean at the commit recorded in each `metadata.json`.
- Runs per target: **S2 3**, **S3 2** (a second run was added to check Bifrost's TTFT step, below; the two runs agree within about 1 ms at p50), **S4 1**, **S5 1** (request size), **S5 response size 1** per size. Single runs have no spread to show; their p99 and max values are noisy (for example, direct's p99 at the same step differs by 1 to 4 ms between runs).
- S3, S4, S5 are not headline scenarios (S3 and S5 are open loop but each has few samples per step, S4 is closed loop). Everything below is k6 client-side measurement, side by side with `direct`; no percentile is subtracted from another.
- Decisions behind the scenario design (rates, chunk counting, payload sizes): D27.

## Observations

**S2 (headline).**
- Highest sustained ladder step (D15, D24): LiteLLM 250 RPS; Bifrost 1000 RPS; Langfuse 500 RPS (1000 held in 2 of 3 runs, the third reached 81 % of the target with 1.7 % errors, so by D24 the lower step is the reported one).
- LiteLLM at 500 and 1000 RPS tops out at about 340 achieved RPS with 2.2 % errors; its p99 reaches the 10 s request timeout. The gateway container is at 200 % CPU (its 2-core limit).
- At the sustained steps, p50 stays near the baseline for Bifrost (62.2 to 63.9 ms against 61.9 to 62.3 direct) and Langfuse (63.2 to 66.3), and sits higher for LiteLLM (68.0 at 100 RPS, 74.0 at 250). The tails differ more than the medians: Langfuse p99 is 94, 186, 200 and 516 ms at 100, 250, 500 and 1000 RPS (medians over the repeats; the 100 RPS p99 alone ranges from 82 to 284 ms between runs), against 64 to 69 ms direct and 65 to 68 ms for Bifrost.
- Resources at 1000 RPS: Bifrost about 59 % of a core and 139 MB; Langfuse's whole stack about 165 % of a core and 2.3 GB, of which the gateway container itself is 39 % and 66 MB (Web carries most of the CPU). LiteLLM is at its 2-core limit at 500 RPS and above.

**S3 (streaming).**
- 50 RPS is the rate every target holds. At 100 RPS LiteLLM is saturated (84 % of the target rate, stream p50 above 1 s); the other three hold it.
- Fidelity is 100 % for every target and run: exactly 40 content chunks, the exact text, finish reason and `[DONE]`, and the usage chunk arrives. No gateway merged, split or dropped chunks (at the SSE event level).
- Time to first content chunk, p50: direct 29.9 ms; LiteLLM 36.5 (p99 143 ms, range 70 to 216; the largest inter-chunk gap reached 192 ms); Bifrost 32.1 at 50 RPS but 43.0 at 100 RPS (both runs, while its stream total is unchanged at about 298 ms; cause not investigated); Langfuse 45.0 and 44.0.
- Inter-chunk gap p50 is 6.8 to 6.9 ms everywhere (the mock's 5 ms plus timer rounding). Gap p99 is 11 to 13 ms for direct, LiteLLM and Bifrost and 8.3 ms for Langfuse. Stream total p50 is 298 to 313 ms for all.

**S4 (closed loop).**
- LiteLLM is the first to flatten: 321 RPS at 100 virtual users (p50 310 ms) and 329 RPS at 1000 (p50 3.0 s, 0.01 % errors, maximum at the 10 s timeout). At 1 and 10 virtual users its p50 is 87 ms, higher than its 68 ms at 100 RPS open loop (S2); not investigated.
- Bifrost scales with the load generator: 1547 RPS at 100 virtual users (p50 63.7 ms) and 8792 RPS at 1000 (p50 111 ms), where its container is at 198 % CPU and k6 at 237 %.
- Langfuse: 1080 RPS at 100 virtual users with 0.77 % errors, and 862 RPS at 1000 with 19.6 % errors (p50 1.1 s). The errors are generated by the gateway: in two short 1000-virtual-user probes on a fresh stack (not committed) the first gave HTTP 504 (`reason="timeout"`, `error.origin="control_plane"`, the gateway's call to Langfuse Web) and the second HTTP 502 `upstream_error` (`provider_transport`) answers. No deeper classification was done.
- `direct` at 1000 virtual users reaches 14,181 RPS with k6 at 365 % CPU, close to the 4-core mark of D17, so that row is nearly load-generator-bound.

**S5 (payload and response size).**
- Every target sustains 100 RPS at 1 KB, 10 KB and 100 KB request bodies. The p50 is nearly flat in body size: LiteLLM 68.1, 68.2, 69.1 ms; Bifrost 63.9, 64.2, 65.4; Langfuse 64.9, 65.0, 65.2; direct 62.4, 62.5, 62.7. CPU grows with body size for LiteLLM (73 to 83 %) and Bifrost (19 to 44 % of a core).
- Langfuse at 10 KB reached 96 RPS (above the 95 % bar) with a p99 of 267 ms: one run, likely run-to-run tail noise (as in S2), not a size effect, since 100 KB did not show it.
- Response size (about 10 KB and 100 KB answers, small request): p50 within 1 ms of the 40-token case for every target.
